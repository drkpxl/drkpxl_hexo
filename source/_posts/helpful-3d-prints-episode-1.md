I've had these two things bugging me for months now and finally got around to fixing them this weekend.

## Missing Window Clips


My basement has these nice EuroTek egress windows. I like leaving them open for airflow, but most of the clips that hold the screen against the window jamb were missing. I had one left, so I measured it up, modeled a replacement in OnShape, and printed what I needed. Took maybe 30 minutes to design and a couple hours to print. Now the screens actually stay in place.

## Soundbar Mount

![Trace](/images/img_2637.jpeg)
![Finished Soundbar](/images/idg_20251004_183432_627-1.jpeg)

After upgrading my main theater to Sonos, I had this old LG soundbar sitting around. Tried to sell it on Facebook Marketplace but nobody wanted it, so I figured I'd throw it in the basement gym. Problem was the only spot to mount it was on ceramic tile, and I didn't want to drill into it.

Sketched out the shape of the soundbar's mounting area, brought the photo into OnShape, modeled a couple brackets, printed them in PETG, and stuck them to the tile with VHB tape. Soundbar sits perfectly on them.

## Wrap Up

It's pretty cool how easy it is to just design and print solutions to these random house problems. Both models are up on [OnShape](https://cad.onshape.com/documents/2b63be830eba06bc1102faa0/w/d3b3727508128bfd03a88a08/e/fc518aa897b9c179a8cbfff3?renderMode=0&uiState=68e3e53f0cc186a85880e9b0) if anyone wants them.

This is exactly the kind of stuff that makes having a 3D printer worthwhile.
